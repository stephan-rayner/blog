Opening

Documentation Isn't Just a Reference Artifact

  • It's trust infrastructure.
  • Its job is to inform, and to preserve the conditions under which people can work together honestly.
5
Without it, power fills the vacuum, and the loudest voice wins.
6
Two Stories

Opposite Ends of the Same Dynamic

  • One where the absence of documentation let power win.
  • One where documentation itself was the power.
7
Story One

Head of Data at a Toxic, Now Defunct Mobile Games Company

  • A team of analysts and data engineers.
  • Stakeholders who wanted data, on their terms.
8

The Newest Person Is the Easiest to Pressure

  • Stakeholders leaned on whoever was junior enough to say yes.
9

Demand Data from the New Analyst

Greg (Head of Business Development) vs. Analina (Junior Analyst)
  • Ask: I want tutorial completion data sliced the following way.
  • Consequence: We are going to be millionaires!!! 🎉🤑
    • 89% D1 in a free-to-play mobile game.
10

No Written Standard, No Cover

  • What data could be shared, in what form, and why: never documented.
  • Every request became a negotiation an analyst had to win alone.
11
The org chart, not the policy, decided who won.
12
Story One

Solution: How We Addressed This

  • Written request filing process in a tool we already used.
  • Event Schema Docs for Stakeholders
    • What this event is.
    • Why we capture this event.
    • What you can't get from this event (common fails).
    • When this event is triggered.
  • This gave the Analysts both a platform and a shield.

The Mechanism

Good Documentation Externalizes Authority

  • It levels a playing field otherwise decided by power, clout, or seniority.
  • Without a difficult conversation every single time.
17
Trading in Trust

We Trade in Trust, Not Documents

  • If people don't trust our documents, it doesn't matter how many we write.
  • If they can't use our documents, they won't trust them.
18
The Framework

Four Ways to Write Docs That Do This Work

  • 1Write for the enforcer, not just the reader
  • 2Anchor to mission rather than procedure
  • 3Document the why alongside the what
  • 4Make your limitations visible
19
1 / 4

Write for the Enforcer, Not Just the Reader

  • The reader wants to know what to do.
  • The enforcer has to hold the line when someone pushes back.
  • Ask: if someone argues, what does the enforcer say next?
20
2 / 4

Anchor to Mission Rather Than Procedure

  • Procedure only covers the cases someone thought of.
  • Mission lets people reason from it when the case isn't covered.
  • Ask: if the steps run out, does the mission tell you what to do next?
21
3 / 4

Document the Why Alongside the What

  • Rules without reasoning invite argument.
  • An unreasoned rule looks arbitrary, and arbitrary rules feel challengeable.
  • ❌ One sentence of reasoning next to every rule.
22
4 / 4

Make Your Limitations Visible

  • A doc that names what it doesn't cover builds more trust than one that oversells.
  • Ask: what doesn't this document cover, and have you said so?
